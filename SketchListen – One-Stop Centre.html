Elements i to v

The Product

What it is, who it is for, what it teaches and what it was built with.

📛

i. Name of the product

SketchListen, a listen-and-connect-the-dots digital innovation for low-proficiency primary listeners.

🎧

ii. Language skill targeted

Listening: understanding the main idea and specific details of short spoken texts, with support. Linked to Year 5 English Content Standard 1.2 and Learning Standards 1.2.1, 1.2.2 and 1.2.3.

📚

iii. Topics addressed

The sample lesson is “Amir and the Kite” (theme: World of Stories, Year 5; 4 parts, 4 questions). Teachers can build new lessons on their own topic, such as celebrations, food or animals.

iv. The aim: the problem SketchListen solves

Audio plays once, in real time. Unlike a reading text, pupils cannot pause it or go back. Weaker listeners spend most of their attention just decoding sounds and words, so little capacity is left to store meaning. The questions that follow depend on memory alone, so many pupils guess, lose confidence and switch off, which makes the next listening task even harder.

Aim: to help low-proficiency Year 5 pupils hold on to what they hear, by turning every short audio segment into a picture and a keyword that pupils build themselves, and by giving teachers a template to create their own listening lessons.

How the idea developed

⏯️

Idea 1: pausable audio player

Gives thinking time, but pupils still keep everything in their heads. Meaning is not stored anywhere.

🧭

Idea 2: listening-strategy platform

Helpful for planning, but weak listeners cannot reflect on audio they cannot yet decode.

✏️

The chosen idea

Combine both: the audio pauses by itself, keywords support decoding, and pupils connect dots to build a picture, an external memory aid made by the pupil.

Element vi

How to Use SketchListen

A step-by-step guide, first from the pupil’s point of view, then from the teacher’s. Screenshots come from the real product.

🧒 For pupils

1Start the lesson. The start screen shows the lesson title, year and theme, and the listening standard. Tap Start.

2Listen, then pause. Press Play to hear one short part of the story. The audio stops by itself.

3Connect the dots. Tap the numbered dots in order. A picture appears and the keyword is revealed. Tap “See my keywords” to continue. No device? Draw the keyword on paper and take a photo.

4Answer the questions. After all parts, the questions appear. The picture cards stay at the bottom as clues, so pupils answer from evidence, not guesses. Tap Check.

5Get instant feedback. Correct answers are ticked and the score appears straight away. Pupils can Play again or go Home.

👩‍🏫 For teachers

1Open the Teacher editor from the start screen. Set the lesson title, year, theme and the listening standard. Choose the options: show the keyword after pupils finish connecting and/or let pupils replay the chunk while connecting.

2Add your audio. Each lesson has up to 6 chunks (one main idea and one keyword each). Record your own voice or attach an audio file, so the pace and accent are familiar to your pupils. Type the transcript and the main keyword.

3Choose the picture to connect. Pick a dotted picture that matches the keyword (kite, tree, ladder, sun, house, bus, book, star, fish, boat) or place your own object.

4Write your questions. Up to 6 questions based on your listening audio: type three answers, tick the correct one and link each question to a chunk.

5Preview and export. Tap Preview as pupil to test the lesson. Use Export lesson file to keep it or share it; another teacher can Import lesson file on their own device.

Elements vii and viii

Features, Impact and Value

vii. Impact of the product for learners

These are the intended impacts, informed by research. They still need to be tested with Malaysian primary pupils.

🧠

Stronger recall

Short, paused chunks match limited working memory (Satori, 2021). The picture and keyword become a memory cue.

🎯

Less guessing

Pupils answer with their pictures still on screen, so answers come from evidence rather than luck.

💪

More confidence

Instant ticks and a score show pupils what they achieved, which can make the next listening task feel less scary.

🔄

Active listening

Connecting the dots keeps pupils engaged while they listen. Research on generative drawing reports a medium-to-large memory benefit (Xie et al., 2024), and a Listen-and-Draw approach improved listening (Nisa & Andriani, 2025).

viii. Novelty and unique features

⏸️

Auto-pause

The audio stops by itself after each part, giving time to process.

🔵

Connect the dots to find the keyword

A guided, low-pressure way to build a picture, with no art skills needed.

🖼️

Picture storyboard stays

The pictures remain visible while pupils answer the questions.

✅

Instant score

Ticks and a score appear straight after checking.

🎙️

Teacher’s own audio

Record your own voice or attach a file, so pace and accent are familiar.

🧩

Reusable template

Choose your own topic, pictures and questions to create a brand-new activity.

⚙️

Flexible options

Show the keyword and/or let pupils replay a chunk, to suit the class.

📤

Export and import

Share a lesson file with another teacher.

📄

Paper backup

No device? Draw the keyword on paper and take a photo.

In the SAMR model, this redesign of the listening task sits at the modification level (Puentedura, 2006).
